How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost in Fort Lauderdale? (2026 Guide)

By BV Consulting•Fort Lauderdale, FL•September 10, 2026•5 min read
Web Design & SEO

"How much does a website cost?" is one of the first questions every small business owner asks, and the honest answer is: it depends. A simple site built yourself might cost a few hundred dollars a year. A custom site from a full-service agency can cost tens of thousands.

This guide breaks down typical 2026 website costs by approach, the ongoing expenses people forget to budget for, what actually drives the price up or down, and what a Fort Lauderdale small business website really needs to bring in customers.

Key takeaways

  • Typical small business websites range from nearly free (DIY) to $35,000+ (full-service agency).
  • Budget for ongoing costs too: domain, hosting, maintenance, security, and updates.
  • The cheapest website is often the most expensive if it never ranks or generates leads.
  • Always get a written quote that lists pages, features, SEO work, and who owns the finished site.

Ongoing costs to budget for

A website isn't a one-time purchase. Plan for these recurring costs:

  • Domain name: often around $10–$20 per year for a .com, and more for some other endings.
  • Hosting: from a few dollars a month for basic shared hosting to much more for managed or high-performance hosting.
  • SSL certificate: often included free with good hosting. Your site should always load with https.
  • Maintenance and security: updates, backups, uptime monitoring, and fixes.
  • Plugins, apps, and integrations: booking tools, forms, e-commerce, or CRM connections.
  • SEO and content: blog posts, local SEO work, and tracking tools.

The same Jim.com analysis estimates total recurring website costs for small businesses at roughly $1,100 to $5,000 per year once you add up hosting, maintenance, security, backups, plugins, and marketing tools.

What makes a website cost more (or less)

  • Number of pages. A five-page brochure site costs less than a site with individual pages for every service and city you serve (which is often better for SEO).
  • Custom design vs. template. Custom design takes more time but helps you stand out and match your brand.
  • Copywriting. Writing clear, persuasive, SEO-friendly content is real work. Some quotes include it, many don't.
  • Features. Online booking, e-commerce, member areas, quote calculators, and multilingual content (common in South Florida) all add scope.
  • Integrations and automation. Connecting forms to your CRM or adding AI follow-up adds cost but can pay for itself quickly.
  • SEO setup. Keyword research, local SEO, schema markup, and Google Business Profile optimization.
  • Photography and branding. Real photos of your team and work beat stock photos, but they need to be created.
  • Redesign and migration. Moving an existing site means redirecting old URLs so you don't lose rankings.

DIY vs. hiring a professional

DIY can make sense if you're just starting, your budget is very tight, and you have time to learn. The trade-off is your time, plus the risk of a site that looks fine but doesn't show up on Google or convert visitors into customers.

Hiring a professional makes sense when your website needs to bring in business. A good web designer isn't just making things look nice. They're planning pages around what customers search for, building trust, and making it easy to call, book, or buy.

The hidden cost of a cheap website

A low price can turn expensive if the site:

  • Loads slowly on phones, so visitors leave before it finishes.
  • Has no local SEO foundation, so it never shows up for searches like "roof repair Fort Lauderdale."
  • Has no clear calls to action, so traffic never turns into calls.
  • Is locked to a provider, so you can't move it without starting over.
  • Needs a full rebuild in a year or two.

Before comparing prices, ask what each quote includes and what results the site is designed to deliver.

What a Fort Lauderdale small business website should include

  • Mobile-first design that's fast on any phone
  • Tap-to-call phone number and a simple contact or quote form on every page
  • Dedicated pages for each main service
  • Clear mention of where you work: Fort Lauderdale, nearby cities, and neighborhoods you serve
  • Your business name, phone number, and location consistent with your Google Business Profile
  • Real reviews, photos, and proof of your work
  • Schema markup that helps search engines understand your business
  • Analytics and Google Search Console so you can see what's working
  • Secure https, accessibility-minded design, and regular backups

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

  1. Who owns the website, domain, and content when the project is done?
  2. What's included: number of pages, copywriting, SEO, forms, and integrations?
  3. How will the site help me show up in local Google searches?
  4. What are the ongoing costs after launch, and what do they cover?
  5. How will I track calls and leads from the site?
  6. What happens if I want changes after launch?

Frequently asked questions

How much does a basic small business website cost?

A basic DIY site can cost very little upfront, while a professionally designed small business site typically ranges from a few thousand dollars with a freelancer to $6,000–$12,000 or more with a boutique agency, based on 2026 industry pricing data.

Are monthly website fees worth it?

They can be, if they cover real value like secure hosting, backups, updates, support, and ongoing SEO. Ask for a clear list of what's included.

How long does it take to build a small business website?

Simple sites can be built in a few weeks. Larger sites with custom features, integrations, or lots of content take longer. Waiting on content and photos is the most common cause of delays.

Is it better to redesign my website or start over?

It depends on your current platform, content quality, and search rankings. If your site already ranks for important searches, a redesign should preserve those pages and redirect any URLs that change.
